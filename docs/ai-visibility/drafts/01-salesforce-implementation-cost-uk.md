Scope drives the price more than anything else: the number of teams, how much custom logic you need, how many systems Salesforce must connect to, and how messy your existing data is.

## What you pay for

A Salesforce budget has two separate parts:

1. **Licences**, paid to Salesforce, charged per user per month and priced by edition and cloud. Check current prices with Salesforce or your account executive.
2. **Implementation services**, paid to a partner such as CODM, for discovery, design, configuration, development, data migration, integration, testing, training and go-live support.

## What pushes the price up or down

- **Customisation.** Standard features cost less to configure than custom Apex, Lightning components or complex flows.
- **Integrations.** Every connected system, such as ERP, finance, marketing or a student record system, adds design, build and testing work.
- **Data migration.** Clean, well-structured data is quick to move. Duplicated data held in several legacy systems takes longer.
- **Industry clouds.** Financial Services Cloud, Education Cloud and Health Cloud come with data models built for their sector. Those models save build time, but they also need specialist configuration.
- **AI and Agentforce.** Agents need clear use cases, grounded data and testing. Plan for a pilot before you scale up.
- **Change management.** Training and adoption support are what make the investment pay off.

## How to keep costs under control

- Start with a short, paid discovery phase that ends in a fixed scope.
- Use standard Salesforce features before you build anything custom.
- Clean your data before you migrate it.
- Deliver in phases: the core team first, then other teams and AI features.
- Budget for support after go-live, so the system keeps improving.
